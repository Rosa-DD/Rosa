🏆 Mut Maker Award
At the end of October I was at BMW Welt as a finalist for the Mut Maker Award, in the category Mut meets Technology. Nina Moghaddam won in our category, warm congratulations to her. And still, I went home that evening stronger than I arrived. Standing up there among so many brave people showed me that the issue we work on every day is being seen. For me, courage right now mostly means saying out loud what stays quiet in so many families: who thinks of what, who plans and who remembers. I am taking that courage into everything that comes next.
🤖 Mousa wins the Industrial Agents Hackathon
Mousa, my co-founder and our CTO, won the Industrial Agents Hackathon in Munich in October. When the news came in, I had goosebumps, and I am so proud of him. It was hosted by Manage and More and UVC Partners, with Anthropic as lead partner and challenges set by Synera, Tacto and Certivity. Around 100 builders had about 24 hours to build AI agents that don’t just look good but actually run. Every day I see how much heart and care Mousa puts into what he builds. That same craft goes into familymind, so your family assistant doesn’t just answer but really takes work off your plate.
🌱 Two Mental Load Shift cohorts have started
In October two groups began our programme The Mental Load Shift with coach Andrea Böckle. Couples learn to make the invisible work at home visible and share it anew, and familymind supports them in everyday life. Seeing how openly participants talk about their everyday life and how quickly they start making changes fills me with deep satisfaction. This is exactly why we built the programme.

The Mental Load Shift
What we are hearing from The Mental Load Shift
Before we started, we asked participants how their everyday life feels right now, and the answers are strikingly similar. Most of them constantly have in their head what is coming up next for the family, many even in the middle of the workday. Even when they hand something over, the feeling stays that they still have to keep track of whether it gets done. And hardly anyone experiences others in the family knowing what needs doing without being told.
That is why what matters most to them is making the invisible work visible and sharing tasks more fairly. When we asked how they would notice after the programme that something had improved, we heard answers like these:
“My head feels lighter.”
“Family members take on tasks by themselves, without me having to follow up.”
“That my partner and I no longer feel the other one always does more than we do.”
After the first check ins, participants say the content fits their life. Two wishes we are taking straight on board: more time for exchange in small groups, and explaining even more clearly how the programme works and where it leads.
That feeling of a head that never clocks off is exactly why we build familymind. And for everyone who wants to find out what truly matters to them as a family before changing anything: on 9 November we will introduce the Family Compass.
